About

I am Professor of Economics and Head of the Department of Economics at the Vienna University of Economics and Business (WU). My research develops econometric methods and applies them to questions in macroeconomics, demographic change, climate change, and human mobility.

A recurring theme in my work is how to draw robust conclusions when model uncertainty is substantial, relationships change over time, and economic outcomes are linked across countries, regions, or sectors. This has led me from methodological work in Bayesian econometrics and forecasting to long-run socioeconomic projections and the quantitative analysis of society–environment interactions.

My research is strongly interdisciplinary and has involved collaborations in economics, demography, geography, sociology, mathematics, and climate science. I am particularly interested in work that connects methodological advances with substantive questions of broad societal relevance.

Current research themes

Econometric methods and forecasting — model uncertainty, structural change, spatial and international interdependencies, and forecast combination.

Demography and long-run development — education, human capital, population dynamics, and socioeconomic projections.

Climate, environment and human mobility — greenhouse-gas emissions, environmental shocks, vulnerability, conflict, and migration.
